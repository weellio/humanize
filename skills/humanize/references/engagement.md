# Engagement layer: LinkedIn, articles, captions

Cleaning prose makes it readable. This makes it spread. Apply after subtract and add, when the goal is reads, comments, and shares, not just clean copy.

## LinkedIn posts

- **Hook line, see-more test.** LinkedIn shows about one line before "see more." That line has to earn the tap on its own. Lead with the sharpest claim or the surprising number. Under ~12 words. Never open with "I'm excited to share" or "In today's world."
- **White space.** One idea per short paragraph, blank line between each. The scroll stops on shape before it stops on words.
- **Take plus stakes.** Three to five short lines. Say what it means and who it hits. Use a first-person reaction: "what surprised me," "the part most people miss," "I'd bet."
- **The question.** End with one honest, opinion-splitting question. Comments push a post past your own followers far more than likes do. A yes/no question dies. "Which side are you on" or "where is the line" travels.
- **Link placement.** Put links in the first comment, not the body. LinkedIn throttles posts that send people off-platform. This is a posting mechanic, not writing, but it changes reach.
- **Hashtags.** Zero to three relevant tags at the end, or none. Stuffing reads as spam.

## Articles

- **The H1 and first paragraph are the hook.** A reader decides to stay or bounce in about two seconds. Open with the specific, surprising thing. Not "In today's fast-paced world."
- **One clear point of view per piece.** An article that only reports gets skimmed. An article that argues gets read and shared. Pick a position and defend it.
- **Specific beats comprehensive.** One vivid number or example lands harder than three vague claims. Cut the survey, keep the story.
- **Subheads that say something.** "What the 40% miss" beats "Analysis." Never "Introduction," "Background," "Conclusion."

## Captions (Instagram, Facebook)

- The first one or two lines carry it. Everything after hides behind "more."
- One hook, one takeaway, one soft prompt. Do not dump the whole article into the caption.

## The engagement check

Before publishing, ask three things:
1. Would the first line alone make me tap "see more"?
2. Is there a real opinion here, or just a summary?
3. Is the closing question one a stranger would actually answer in public?

If any answer is no, the post is clean but it will not travel.
