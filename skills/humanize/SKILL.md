# Humanize

Two moves, and you always do both:

1. **Subtract** the AI tells (clean the writing).
2. **Add** the human (voice, opinion, specificity, stakes).

Removal alone leaves clean, correct, forgettable prose. Nobody comments on forgettable. Engagement comes from what you add: a real take, a number, a stake, a voice the reader recognizes. Subtract without adding and you get "clean but soulless." Do both, every time.

## Workflow

**0. Calibrate voice (optional, powerful).** If a writing sample exists, read it for four things and mirror them: sentence-length pattern, word-choice level, punctuation habits, recurring tics. Then replace AI patterns with the sample's patterns, not generic ones. Detail: [references/voice.md](references/voice.md).

**1. Subtract the tells.** Apply the eight removal rules below. Full catalogs: [references/remove-phrases.md](references/remove-phrases.md), [references/remove-structures.md](references/remove-structures.md).

**2. Add the human.** Inject the three substance moves below. Detail and examples: [references/voice.md](references/voice.md).

**3. Engagement pass** (posts, articles, captions). Hook, stakes, one real question. See [references/engagement.md](references/engagement.md).

**4. Self-audit.** Ask: "What still makes this obviously AI-generated?" Hunt for tidy rhythm, surviving signposting, three-item lists that snuck back, slogan-y closers. Then: "Now make it not obviously AI-generated," and revise.

**5. Score /80.** Eight dimensions: five mechanics, three substance. Below 56, or any dimension under its floor, revise. If mechanics score high but substance lags, it is clean but soulless. Return to step 2.

## Subtract: the eight rules

1. Cut filler. Throat-clearing openers ("Here's the thing"), emphasis crutches ("Let that sink in"), and all adverbs (really, just, genuinely, honestly). See remove-phrases.
2. Break formulaic structures. No binary contrasts ("not X, it's Y"), no negative listing, no dramatic fragments. See remove-structures.
3. Active voice. Every sentence has a human subject doing something. No inanimate thing doing a human verb ("the data tells us" becomes someone read it).
4. Be specific. No vague declaratives ("the implications are significant"). Name the thing.
5. Put the reader in the room. "You" beats "people." No narrator floating above the scene.
6. Vary rhythm. Mix sentence lengths. Two items beat three. No em dashes or en dashes (ranges use a hyphen).
7. Trust readers. State facts directly. Skip softening and hand-holding.
8. Cut quotables. If a line sounds like a pull-quote, rewrite it.

## Add: the three substance moves

**Specificity.** Real names, numbers, places, the actual thing. Not "industry observers note" but who, when, where. Not "a range of accuracy" but "40% on one detector, 0% on another." A number is a magnet; a category is wallpaper.

**Restraint.** State things at their real size. Cut significance inflation ("stands as a testament," "marks a pivotal moment," "game-changer"). Say what happened and let the reader rank it.

**Voice.** Have an opinion. React to a fact instead of only reporting it. Use "I" when it is honest (first person reads as honest, not unprofessional). Name the feeling precisely: not "this is concerning" but "there's something off about a tool that flags every human as a bot." A point of view is the line between wire-copy and a person talking.

## Engagement layer (posts and articles)

For anything meant to earn reads and comments (full playbook in [references/engagement.md](references/engagement.md)):

- **Hook first.** Line one must stand alone and earn the click. On LinkedIn everything after line one hides behind "see more." Lead with the sharpest claim or number. Under ~12 words. No "excited to share."
- **White space.** One idea per short paragraph, blank line between. Thumb-stopping is visual before it is verbal.
- **A take with stakes.** Say what it means and who it hits. First-person reaction beats neutral summary.
- **End on a real question.** One honest, opinion-splitting question. Comments spread a post past your followers far more than likes. Avoid yes/no; ask which side, or where the line is.
- **First person, practitioner voice.** A person with a stake, not a brand desk.

## Scoring

| Dimension | Type | Low | High | Floor |
|-----------|------|-----|------|-------|
| Directness | mechanics | Announces | States | 5 |
| Rhythm | mechanics | Metronomic | Varied | 5 |
| Trust | mechanics | Hand-holds | Respects reader | 5 |
| Authenticity | mechanics | Reads as AI | Reads as human | 5 |
| Density | mechanics | Padded | Nothing cuttable | 5 |
| Specificity | substance | Vague categories | Names the thing | 6 |
| Restraint | substance | Puffed up | Actual size | 5 |
| Voice | substance | Neutral wire-copy | Has a point of view | 6 |

Pass at 56/80 with no dimension below its floor. Substance floors sit higher on purpose. That is where engagement lives.
